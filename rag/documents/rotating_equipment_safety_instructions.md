# SI-11 — Rotating Equipment High-Vibration and Repeated-Trip Response

## 1. Purpose

This safety instruction applies to all rotating equipment (pumps, compressors,
motors) across all units and takes precedence over asset-specific operating
procedures where the two conflict.

## 2. High Vibration — Critical Setpoint

Rotating equipment operating at or above its critical vibration setpoint
must not be intentionally run to failure "to keep the process running."
Continuing to operate a machine in this condition risks catastrophic bearing
or coupling failure, potential shaft ejection, and secondary damage to
adjacent piping and instrumentation.

- If vibration is at or above the critical (not just high) setpoint, the
  equipment must be removed from service as soon as a safe transfer to a
  standby unit (or a controlled process shutdown, if no standby exists) can
  be completed — normally within 15 minutes of the alarm.
- A "ride it out" decision on a critical vibration alarm requires shift
  supervisor and reliability engineer sign-off, logged in the shift log,
  and is only permitted when continued operation is assessed as lower risk
  than an uncontrolled process upset from an immediate shutdown.

## 3. Repeated Protective Trips

A protective trip (motor trip, low lube oil pressure trip, etc.) that recurs
twice within 30 minutes on the same root cause must not be reset a third
time without electrical or mechanical engineering sign-off, regardless of
production pressure. Protective relays and interlocks exist specifically to
prevent equipment damage or a safety event; repeated bypassing through
uninvestigated resets defeats their purpose.

## 4. General Principle

When in doubt, treat vibration and repeated-trip alarms as equipment
integrity issues first and production issues second. A short controlled
outage is preferable to an uncontrolled failure.
